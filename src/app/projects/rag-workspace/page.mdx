  <CaseStudySection id="summary" number="1" title="Executive summary">
    Financial filings are long, revised over time, and hostile to naive retrieval. FinDoc imports SEC EDGAR filings, parses and indexes structured sections, stores embeddings with their filing metadata, retrieves evidence progressively, and returns answers grounded in citations.
  </CaseStudySection>

  <CaseStudySection id="problem" number="2" title="The problem, drawn" className="mt-14">
    An annual report is not one document. A 10-K/A amends particular sections of its parent filing and leaves the rest standing. Treating the newest file as complete produces confident answers from an incomplete document; treating every filing independently can cite text that has been revised.
    <TechnicalFigure number="1" caption="Amendments supersede parts, not whole filings"><AmendmentLineageFigure /></TechnicalFigure>
  </CaseStudySection>

  <CaseStudySection id="system" number="3" title="System" className="mt-14">
    Ingestion pulls filings from EDGAR, Docling parses them into structured sections, and chunks are embedded into pgvector alongside filing metadata. Retrieval runs in passes and stops when the evidence is sufficient; generation sees only text that can be cited.
    <TechnicalFigure number="2" caption="Ingestion to cited answer"><RetrievalPipelineFigure /></TechnicalFigure>
  </CaseStudySection>

  <CaseStudySection id="decisions" number="4" title="Decisions I would defend in review" className="mt-14">
    <div className="space-y-8">
      <DecisionBlock number="1" title="Amendment lineage over latest-wins" note="Cost: query-time complexity. Worth it.">A 10-K/A supersedes parts of its parent, not the entire document. Lineage is resolved at query time rather than flattened at ingestion, so the system can answer from the applicable version.</DecisionBlock>
      <DecisionBlock number="2" title="Idempotent imports keyed on accession number">Re-running an import is safe. That makes backfills and parser reprocessing routine rather than a source of duplicate records and ambiguous provenance.</DecisionBlock>
      <DecisionBlock number="3" title="Progressive retrieval before generation">Cheaper passes run first; more expensive passes are used only when the evidence remains thin. The point is grounding, not a model call for its own sake.</DecisionBlock>
    </div>
